\author{Palash Pandya}

\begin{abstract}
Absolutely maximally entangled states of four six-level systems, AME$(4,6)$, are the quantum solutions of Euler's
problem of the thirty-six officers.  All solutions reported so far are complex, and it has been conjectured that no
real orthogonal 2-unitary matrix of order $36$ exists.  We give real AME$(4,6)$ states with $60$ non-zero
coefficients in $\{\pm1,\sqrt2\}/\sqrt{72}$.  They lie in the $-1$
eigenspace of an order-$4$ local symmetry, and on its support the AME conditions become linear.  We derive two closed-form families of AME$(4,6)$ states: a $10$-parameter family that
%contains 2-unitary complex weighing matrices and
is supported on $72$ basis states, and an $11$-parameter family on $60$ basis states, that meet at the real state.  At each of its two ends the first family loses twelve coefficients, and the AME states on the remaining $60$ basis states
form an $11$-parameter family: the second family at one end, and an analogous family at the other.  Together these two contain $4096$ real AME states up to local phases, $1024$ of which are limits of the first family.  They fall into exactly $20$ classes under local unitaries and permutations of the parties, and we certify their separation with interval arithmetic.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A pure state of $N$ parties with $d$ levels each is absolutely maximally entangled, AME$(N,d)$, if every reduction
to $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ parties is maximally mixed~\cite{Helwig2012,GALRZ15}.  Such states are perfect tensors; they
underlie quantum secret sharing~\cite{Helwig2012} and holographic codes~\cite{PYHP2015}, and 2-unitary matrices
are dual-unitary gates of maximal entangling power~\cite{RAL20,BKP2019}.  For four parties, writing $U_{(ij),(kl)}=d\,\psi_{ijkl}$, the state is AME if and only if
$U$ is \emph{2-unitary}: $U$, its realignment $U^R$ and its partial transpose $U^\Gamma$ are all
unitary~\cite{GALRZ15}.  Up to permutations of rows and columns, these three matrices are the reshapings of
$d\,\psi$ along the three bipartitions $12|34$, $13|24$ and $14|23$.

A pair of orthogonal Latin squares of order $d$ gives a 2-unitary permutation matrix.  Such pairs exist for every
$d\ge3$ except $d=6$~\cite{BSP1960}: Euler's problem of the $36$ officers has no
solution~\cite{Euler1782,Tarry1900}.  AME$(4,2)$ states do not exist at all~\cite{HS2000}.  An AME$(4,6)$ state, a
quantum solution of Euler's problem, was found by Rather \emph{et al.}~\cite{RBB22}.  Further solutions include
continuous families obtained from it~\cite{RRKL23}, 2-unitary complex Hadamard matrices~\cite{BZ24}, constructions
from biunimodular vectors~\cite{Rat24} and hand-made solutions~\cite{GG25}.  No pure stabilizer AME$(4,6)$ state
exists~\cite{WMBA26,Cha26}, and every quantum solution requires entanglement: mutually orthogonal quantum Latin
squares of order six without entanglement do not exist~\cite{BS26}.

All of these solutions are complex.  In the Supplemental Material of Ref.~\cite{RBB22} the authors write:
``Further numerical results suggest that any 2-unitary matrix $U_{36}$ has complex entries.  Therefore, it is
tempting to conjecture that there exists no solution in the set $O(36)$ of orthogonal matrices of this size.''  A
recent review lists the question as open problem (T2)~\cite{RBRLZ26}: ``Is there an AME(4,6) state with real
coefficients? Equivalently, is there an orthogonal 2-unitary matrix of order 36?''

We answer this question in the affirmative.  With the permutation solutions for $d\neq2,6$, it follows that a real
AME$(4,d)$ state exists if and only if $d\ge3$, exactly when a complex one does. We present the following results in this work.
In \autoref{sec:state} is our central result: an explicit real AME$(4,6)$ state with $60$ non-zero coefficients, the sparsest AME$(4,6)$ state we know of,
whose 2-unitary representation consists of direct sums of $1\times1$ and $2\times2$ blocks.
In \autoref{sec:symmetry} we describe its origin. It lies in the $-1$ eigenspace of the local symmetry
$u^{\otimes4}$, $u=\mathrm{diag}(i,-1,-1,-i,-i,-i)$, and we show that the AME conditions are linear on its support.
In addition, \autoref{sec:families} two continuous families of AME$(4,6)$ states are presented in closed form, with supports on 72 and 60 basis states. The two families meet at the real state. In \autoref{sec:real} we show that the families allow a complete classification of the real AME states with the two sixty-term supports at the ends of the support-72 family: $4096$ states in exactly twenty classes under local unitaries and party permutations, all of them isolated (numerically).
All statements are verified by self-contained scripts~\cite{repo}.

\section{A real AME(4,6) state}\label{sec:state}

If we index the basis states of each party as $\ket{i}$, where $i = 0,\dots,5$, then we can write the state as array of Euler's officers where the first two
parties carry rank $a$ and regiment $b$, and the last two parties carry the state $\ket{\phi_{ab}}$ attached to them:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state}
\ket\psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{72}}\sum_{a,b=0}^{5}\ket{ab}\otimes\ket{\phi_{ab}}\,.
\end{equation}

The $36$ states $\ket{\phi_{ab}}$, for one officer each, are listed in Table~\ref{tab:state}.
The $12$ shaded cells are \emph{classical} officers: a single
basis state with coefficient $\sqrt2/\sqrt{72}=1/6$.  The other $24$ are \emph{entangled} officers, each a
superposition of two basis states with coefficients $\pm1/\sqrt{72}$.  The support of $\ket\psi$ thus has
$12+2\cdot24=60$ basis states $\ket x=\ket{x_1x_2x_3x_4}$, where $x_j\in\{0,\dots,5\}$ is the index of party $j$.
With $\psi_x=\braket{x}{\psi}$ we write
\[
P=\{x:\psi_x=1/6\},\qquad E=\{x:\psi_x=\pm1/\sqrt{72}\}
\]
for the $12$ basis states of the classical officers ($P$) and the $48$ of the entangled ones ($E$).
%, and $\psi_x=\varepsilon_x/\sqrt{72}$ with $\varepsilon_x=\pm1$ for $x\in E$ and $\varepsilon_x=\sqrt{2}$ for $x\in P$.
For $x\in P$ all four indices $x_j$ lie in $\{0,3,4,5\}$ and exactly one of them is $0$, e.g.\ $\ket{0344}$;
for $x\in E$ exactly two of them lie in $\{1,2\}$, e.g.\ $\ket{0012}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:state}
$\ket\psi$ is an AME$(4,6)$ state: The reshaped matrix $U_{(ij),(kl)} = 6(\ket\psi)_{ijkl}$ is a real orthogonal 2-unitary matrix of order $36$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
%Fix a bipartition and let $U$ be the corresponding reshaping of $6\psi$.
We first show that $U$ is orthogonal, i.e.\ that the bipartition $12|34$ is maximally entangled:
$\rho_{12}=UU^T/36=\mathbb I/36$.  The row $(a,b)$ of $U$ is the officer $\ket{\phi_{ab}}/\sqrt2$ of
Table~\ref{tab:state}, written in the basis $\ket{x_3x_4}$ of parties $3,4$.  A classical officer gives a row with one
entry $1$, an entangled officer a row with two entries $\pm1/\sqrt2$.  Every ket $\ket{x_3x_4}$ appears either in
exactly one cell, or in exactly two cells that contain the same two kets.  The rows of two such cells are therefore
supported on the same two columns, which no other row uses, and after permuting rows and columns $U$ is a direct sum of
$12$ blocks $(1)$ and $12$ blocks $\frac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}s_1&s_2\\ s_3&s_4\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
with $s_i=\pm1$; e.g.\ $(a,b)=(0,0)$ and $(5,5)$ give
$\frac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&1\\ 1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.  The two officers of a pair are
orthogonal, $s_1s_3+s_2s_4=0$, so every block, and hence $U$, is orthogonal.

The same argument applies to $U^R$ and $U^\Gamma$, the reshapings along $13|24$ and $14|23$, whose rows collect the
basis states $\ket x$ with given indices $(x_1,x_3)$ or $(x_1,x_4)$.  Each of them again has $12$ rows with one entry
$1$ and $24$ rows with two entries $\pm1/\sqrt2$, paired on the same two columns with orthogonal signs, so both are
direct sums of $12$ blocks $(1)$ and $12$ orthogonal $2\times2$ blocks; their block lists are part of the exact check
in Ref.~\cite{repo}.  Hence also $\rho_{13}=\rho_{14}=\mathbb I/36$, and the complementary pairs follow because
$\ket\psi$ is pure.  So $U$ is 2-unitary and $\ket\psi$ is AME.
\end{proof}

All one- and two-party marginals have also been verified in exact arithmetic over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$~\cite{repo};
Fig.~\ref{fig:amplitudes} shows the three 2-unitaries.  In the golden AME solution all
$36$ officers are entangled~\cite{RBB22}.  The classical officers also make $\ket\psi$ as close to a product
state as an AME$(4,6)$ state can be: for every product state $\ket v=\ket{v_1v_2v_3v_4}$,
$|\langle v|\psi\rangle|^2\le\langle v_1v_2|\rho_{12}|v_1v_2\rangle=1/36$, with equality at the $12$ states of $P$.

\section{Symmetry and linearization}\label{sec:symmetry}

The state was obtained as a byproduct of a search
%that takes only $N=4$ and $d=6$ as input.  We looked
for AME states in eigenspaces
of local symmetries $u^{\otimes4}$, $u=VDV^\dagger$, with $V$ Haar random and $D$ diagonal of finite order.  Two
necessary conditions restrict $D$ and the eigenvalue $\lambda$.  First, if $u^{\otimes4}\ket\psi=\lambda\ket\psi$, the
reshaping of $\ket\psi$ along a cut $A|A^c$ connects the $\mu$-eigenspace of $u^{\otimes|A|}$ only with the
$\lambda\bar\mu$-eigenspace of $u^{\otimes|A^c|}$.
%For $|A|=2$ the reshaping is proportional to a unitary, so these
%eigenspaces must have equal dimensions for every $\mu$; for $|A|=1$ the second must be at least as large.
Second, in the eigenbasis of $u$ the populations $|\psi_x|^2$ must form a probability distribution on the basis states of the
eigenspace with uniform two-party marginals (these are the diagonals of $\rho_{jk}=\mathbb I/36$), which is formulated as a linear feasibility problem.
%Up to order six,
%$121$ of the $204$ symmetry classes that pass the first condition also pass the second~\cite{repo}.
In particular, with complex unit $i$, take $V=\mathbb I$ and $u=D=\mathrm{diag}(i^{k_0},\dots,i^{k_5})$ with $k=(1,2,2,3,3,3)$.
The $-1$ eigenspace of $u^{\otimes4}$ is spanned by the $360$ basis states $\ket{x_1x_2x_3x_4}$ with $k_{x_1}+\dots+k_{x_4}\equiv2\pmod4$, and a random-start optimization
in it finds AME states within a few restarts.  Most of them have a $10$-dimensional space of first-order deformations
modulo local unitaries (\autoref{sec:families}), the others a $3$-dimensional one.\footnote{A first-order deformation
of an AME state $\ket\psi$ is a vector $\ket{\delta\psi}$ such that the two-party marginals of $\ket\psi+t\ket{\delta\psi}$
remain $\mathbb I/36$ to first order in $t$; for four parties it suffices to check $\rho_{12}$, $\rho_{13}$ and
$\rho_{14}$.  These vectors form a real vector space.  It always contains the tangent space of the local-unitary orbit,
spanned by the vectors $i(H_1\otimes\mathbb I\otimes\mathbb I\otimes\mathbb I+\dots+\mathbb I\otimes\mathbb I\otimes\mathbb
I\otimes H_4)\ket\psi$ with Hermitian $H_j$, and the dimension of the quotient is invariant under local unitaries.  It
is an upper bound on the number of parameters, modulo local unitaries, of any family of AME states through $\ket\psi$,
with equality where the rank of the linearized conditions is locally constant.}
Local unitaries that commute with $u$, namely $U(1)\times U(2)\times U(3)$ acting on the basis states
$\ket0$; $\ket1,\ket2$; and $\ket3,\ket4,\ket5$ of each party, preserve the eigenspace.  Minimizing a smoothed $\ell_1$
norm of the coefficients over such changes of local basis can bring a solution of the first kind to exactly $72$
non-zero coefficients. A subsequent Gauss--Newton optimization on that support makes it AME to machine precision~\cite{repo}.  These $72$ basis states form an orthogonal array of strength $2$ and index
$2$~\cite{HSS1999}.%: for any two parties $j\neq k$ and any $m,m'\in\{0,\dots,5\}$, exactly two of the $72$ basis states
%have $x_j=m$ and $x_k=m'$.
They consist of the $60$ basis states $E\cup P$ of the support of $\ket\psi$ and $12$ further ones, which we call $P'$
(listed in Eq.~\eqref{eq:quartets}); as for $P$, all four indices of each $x\in P'$ lie in $\{0,3,4,5\}$ and exactly one of them is
$0$.  Altogether, the $72$ basis states lie in the $-1$ eigenspace ($\sum_jk_{x_j}\equiv2\pmod4$ for each), so every state supported on them, including
$\ket\psi$ and both families of \autoref{sec:families}, satisfies $u^{\otimes4}\ket\psi=-\ket\psi$.  Modulo local
phases, the diagonal local unitaries that act on this support as a scalar form the group
$\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_4$, in which $u^{\otimes4}$ has order $4$.

In each of the three reshapings, the rows and the columns are labeled by the indices of two complementary pairs of
parties, so strength $2$ and index $2$ mean that every row and every column has exactly two non-zero entries.
Moreover, two rows that share a column share both of their columns, so after permuting rows and columns each reshaping
is a direct sum of $18$ full $2\times2$ blocks~\cite{repo}: the $12$ blocks of $E$ (the \emph{$E$-blocks}) from the
proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:state}, and six \emph{mixed} blocks with two states of $P$ on one diagonal and two of
$P'$ on the other.  Over the three reshapings this gives $36$ $E$-blocks and $18$ mixed blocks.  A
block-diagonal matrix is unitary if and only if each of its blocks is, and for a $2\times2$ block this amounts to
linear equations in the squared moduli and a single linear congruence in the phases: on such a support the AME
conditions linearize.  On the support $E\cup P$ of $\ket\psi$ the six mixed blocks split into $12$ blocks of size
$1\times1$, and the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:state} is this argument.  Allowing both kinds of blocks gives the
following lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:blocks}
Let $\ket\psi=\sum_{x\in S}\psi_x\ket x$ with $\psi_x=\sqrt{s_x/72}\,e^{i\varphi_x}$, where $s_x>0$ and
$\varphi_x\in\mathbb R$.  In each of the three reshapings $U$, $U^R$, $U^\Gamma$ of $6\ket\psi$, along $12|34$,
$13|24$ and $14|23$, the basis state $\ket x$ lies in the row labeled by its indices for the first pair of parties and
in the column labeled by those for the second; for $x\in S$ in row $r$ and column $c$ we write $s_{rc}=s_x$ and
$\varphi_{rc}=\varphi_x$.  Suppose that in each reshaping the $36$ rows and the $36$ columns can be partitioned into
sets $R_1,\dots,R_m$ and $C_1,\dots,C_m$ with $|R_k|=|C_k|\in\{1,2\}$ such that $S$ consists exactly of the basis
states in $R_1\times C_1,\dots,R_m\times C_m$, the \emph{blocks} of $S$.  Then $\ket\psi$ is AME if and only if
$s_x=2$ on every $1\times1$ block and every $2\times2$ block, with rows $r,r'$ and columns $c,c'$, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:congruence}
\begin{gathered}
s_{rc}=s_{r'c'},\quad s_{rc'}=s_{r'c},\quad s_{rc}+s_{rc'}=2,\\
\varphi_{rc}-\varphi_{rc'}-\varphi_{r'c}+\varphi_{r'c'}\equiv\pi\pmod{2\pi}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In particular, if these moduli equations have a solution with all $s_x>0$, a real AME state with support $S$ exists
if and only if the equations $t_{rc}+t_{rc'}+t_{r'c}+t_{r'c'}=1$, one for each $2\times2$ block, have a solution
$t\in\mathbb F_2^S$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The reduced state of parties $1,2$ is $\rho_{12}=UU^\dagger/36$, and that of parties $3,4$ is
$\rho_{34}=\overline{U^\dagger U}/36$; likewise for $U^R$ and $U^\Gamma$.  Hence $\ket\psi$ is AME, i.e.\ all six
two-party reductions equal $\mathbb I/36$ (which includes $\braket{\psi}{\psi}=1$), if and only if $U$, $U^R$ and
$U^\Gamma$ are unitary.

Fix one reshaping $U$ and its blocks.  The entry of $U$ in row $r$ and column $c$ is $\sqrt{s_{rc}/2}\,e^{i\varphi_{rc}}$
if the basis state there lies in $S$, and $0$ otherwise.  Since the $R_k$ and the $C_k$ partition the rows and the
columns and all entries of $S$ lie in the blocks $R_k\times C_k$, ordering the rows as $R_1,\dots,R_m$ and the columns
as $C_1,\dots,C_m$ makes $U$ block diagonal, with diagonal blocks $B_k$ the submatrices on $R_k\times C_k$.  So $U$ is unitary if and only if every $B_k$ is.  A $1\times1$ block is unitary if and
only if $s_x/2=1$.  A $2\times2$ block $B$ with rows $r,r'$ and columns $c,c'$ is unitary if and only if its rows are
orthonormal:
\[
s_{rc}+s_{rc'}=s_{r'c}+s_{r'c'}=2,\qquad B_{rc}\bar B_{r'c}+B_{rc'}\bar B_{r'c'}=0.
\]
Then $B^\dagger B=\mathbb I$ as well, so also $s_{rc}+s_{r'c}=s_{rc'}+s_{r'c'}=2$.  These four norm equations are
equivalent to the three moduli equations in~\eqref{eq:congruence}.  Under them, the two terms of the orthogonality
condition have the same modulus $\sqrt{s_{rc}s_{rc'}}/2>0$, so their sum vanishes if and only if their phases
$\varphi_{rc}-\varphi_{r'c}$ and $\varphi_{rc'}-\varphi_{r'c'}$ differ by $\pi$ modulo $2\pi$, which is the congruence.
Conversely, the moduli equations and the congruence give orthonormal rows.  So $B$ is unitary if and only if
Eq.~\eqref{eq:congruence} holds; exchanging $r$ with $r'$, or $c$ with $c'$, gives an equivalent system, so the
labeling within a block does not matter.  Collecting the conditions of all blocks of the three reshapings proves the
first claim.

The moduli equations involve only $s$ and the congruences only $\varphi$, so any positive solution $s$ and any
solution $\varphi$ together give an AME state with support $S$, and every such state arises in this way.  It is real
if and only if all $\varphi_x\in\{0,\pi\}$.  Writing $\varphi_x=\pi t_x$ with $t_x\in\{0,1\}$, the congruence becomes
$t_{rc}-t_{rc'}-t_{r'c}+t_{r'c'}\equiv1\pmod2$, which is the stated equation over $\mathbb F_2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Two families through the real state}\label{sec:families}

We now describe, up to diagonal local unitaries, all AME states with support exactly $E\cup P\cup P'$, $E\cup P$ or
$E\cup P'$.  Let $\varepsilon_x=\pm1$ be the sign of $\psi_x$ for $x\in E$.  For $\delta\in[-1,1]$ and phases
$\alpha_x$ ($x\in P$) and $\beta_x$ ($x\in P'$) let
\begin{multline}\label{eq:family}
\ket{\Psi_\delta(\alpha,\beta)}=\frac1{\sqrt{72}}\Big[\sum_{x\in E}\varepsilon_x\ket x\\
+\sqrt{1+\delta}\sum_{x\in P}e^{i\alpha_x}\ket x+\sqrt{1-\delta}\sum_{x\in P'}e^{i\beta_x}\ket x\Big].
\end{multline}
At $\delta=1$ the coefficients on $P'$ vanish, so the phases $\beta$ play no role and we write $\ket{\Psi_1(\alpha)}$;
likewise, at $\delta=-1$ the phases $\alpha$ play no role and we write $\ket{\Psi_{-1}(\beta)}$.  A number in place of
$\alpha$ or $\beta$ means that all phases on $P$, or all phases on $P'$, have that value.  Thus, the real state in Eq.~\ref{eq:state} is $\ket\psi=\ket{\Psi_1(0)}$,
while the other end with all phases zero is a different real AME state,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psiminus}
\ket{\psi_-}=\ket{\Psi_{-1}(0)}=\frac1{\sqrt{72}}\sum_{x\in E}\varepsilon_x\ket x+\frac16\sum_{x\in P'}\ket x ,
\end{equation}
with the twelve coefficients $1/6$ on $P'$ instead of $P$.  No local unitary and permutation of the parties maps
$\ket\psi$ to $\ket{\psi_-}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:classes}).  Each of the
$18$ mixed blocks has two states $a,b\in P$ on one diagonal and two states $a',b'\in P'$ on
the other.  For $|\delta|<1$ the moduli of Eq.~\eqref{eq:family} satisfy the moduli equations of
Lemma~\ref{lem:blocks} on every block, and the $E$-blocks carry the coefficients of $\ket\psi$ which satisfy their respective
phase congruences.  The only remaining AME conditions are therefore the phase congruences~\eqref{eq:congruence} of the
mixed blocks, which read
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed}
\alpha_a+\alpha_b\equiv\beta_{a'}+\beta_{b'}+\pi\pmod{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
The $18$ pairs $\{a,b\}$ are the pairs of states within three quartets $P_1,P_2,P_3$ that partition $P$, each pair
occurring in exactly one mixed block, and likewise the pairs $\{a',b'\}$ for three quartets $P'_1,P'_2,P'_3$ of $P'$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quartets}
\input{inputs/family_sets.tex}
\end{equation}
The mixed blocks join the quartets in a regular way: for all $k$ and $l$, exactly two of them contain states of $P_k$
and $P'_l$, and their pairs split $P_k$ into two pairs $p,\bar p$ and $P'_l$ into two pairs $q,\bar q$, where partner
pairs have the same first indices~\cite{repo}.  With $\alpha(p)$ the sum of $\alpha$ over $p$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed}
for these two blocks reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition}
\alpha(p)\equiv\beta(q)+\pi,\qquad\alpha(\bar p)\equiv\beta(\bar q)+\pi.
\end{equation}
We call the AME states with support exactly $E\cup P$ or $E\cup P'$ the two \emph{faces},
$\mathcal F_{60}$ and $\mathcal F_{60}'$.  Eq.~\eqref{eq:family} covers both faces at $\delta=\pm1$, but a face is more
than the set of limits of the states with $|\delta|<1$: by Proposition~\ref{prop:families}(c), the AME states with
support $E\cup P\cup P'$ reach each face only along a $9$-dimensional subtorus.

$\mathcal F_{72}^+$ from a uniform state with four imaginary coefficients to $\ket\psi$.}
\label{fig:families}
\end{figure}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:families}
(a) Up to diagonal local unitaries, the AME states with support exactly $E\cup P\cup P'$ are the states
$\ket{\Psi_\delta(\alpha,\beta)}$ with $|\delta|<1$ that satisfy Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed}.  They form two components
$\mathcal F_{72}^\pm\ni\ket{\Psi_\delta(0,\pm\pi/2)}$, exchanged by complex conjugation, with $10$ parameters each.\\
(b) Likewise, those with support exactly $E\cup P$ are the states $\ket{\Psi_1(\alpha)}$, with $11$ parameters, and
those with support exactly $E\cup P'$ the states $\ket{\Psi_{-1}(\beta)}$.\\
(c) $\ket{\Psi_1(\alpha)}$ is a limit of $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$ if and only if the sums of $\alpha$ over $P_1$, $P_2$
and $P_3$ agree modulo $2\pi$; likewise for $\ket{\Psi_{-1}(\beta)}$.\\
(d) No state of $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$ is real, even up to diagonal local unitaries.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:blocks}, everything reduces to linear congruences in the phases; the ranks and invariant factors
quoted below are computed exactly in Ref.~\cite{repo}.  We use two facts.  (i) If a system of integer linear
congruences in $n$ phases has rank $\rho$ and invariant factors $d_1,\dots,d_\rho$ (the non-zero entries of the Smith
normal form of its matrix), its solutions, if any, form $d_1\cdots d_\rho$ disjoint $(n-\rho)$-dimensional tori.
(ii) In each reshaping, a diagonal local unitary adds to $\varphi_x$ a function of the row of $x$ plus a function of
its column, which cancels in every congruence~\eqref{eq:congruence}.

To tell the two components apart we use
\[
\omega=\sum_{x\in P',\,x_1\neq0}\varphi_x-\sum_{x\in P,\,x_1\neq0}\varphi_x .
\]
The nine mixed blocks without a state of first index $0$ contain each of these $18$ states exactly twice, so adding
their congruences in the form~\eqref{eq:mixed} gives $2\omega\equiv\pi$: every AME state with support $E\cup P\cup P'$
has $\omega\equiv\pm\pi/2$.  Diagonal local unitaries form a connected group that maps such states to such states, so
they cannot change this discrete value; complex conjugation reverses it.

(a) The moduli equations of the $E$-blocks have rank $48$, so $s=1$ on $E$.  On a mixed block they give $s_a=s_b$,
$s_{a'}=s_{b'}$ and $s_a+s_{a'}=2$.  Every pair within a quartet lies in a mixed block, so $s$ is constant on each
quartet, and by Eq.~\eqref{eq:partition} every $P_k$ shares a mixed block with every $P'_l$, so $s(P_k)+s(P'_l)=2$.
Hence $s=1+\delta$ on $P$ and $1-\delta$ on $P'$, with $|\delta|<1$ as $s>0$.  The $36$ congruences of the
$E$-blocks have rank $28$ in $48$ phases and invariant factors $1$, so by (i) their solutions form one $20$-dimensional
torus.  By (ii) it contains the phases of $\ket\psi$ shifted by any diagonal local unitary, which also form a
$20$-dimensional torus, so the two coincide.  Hence a diagonal local unitary brings every AME state with this support to some
$\ket{\Psi_\delta(\alpha,\beta)}$, and such a state is AME if and only if Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed} holds.  This system has
rank $14$ in $24$ phases and invariant factors $1,\dots,1,2$, so by (i) its solutions form two disjoint
$10$-dimensional tori.  At $(\alpha,\beta)=(0,\pm\pi/2)$, $\omega=\pm9\pi/2\equiv\pm\pi/2$, so $\omega$ tells the two
tori apart; together with $\delta$ they give $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$, on which $\omega\equiv\pm\pi/2$.  Complex
conjugation, $(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(-\alpha,-\beta)$, exchanges them, and no diagonal local unitary does.  A diagonal
local unitary that maps a state of the family to another must fix the coefficients on $E$; on $P\cup P'$ it then acts
by a common phase, possibly combined with the sign change of $P_3\cup P'$.  The common phase removes one of the $10$
phase dimensions, leaving $9$ parameters besides $\delta$.

(b) On $E\cup P$ the $1\times1$ blocks give $s=2$ on $P$ and no phase condition, so the states are
$\ket{\Psi_1(\alpha)}$ for all $\alpha$; the common phase leaves $11$ parameters.  The same holds on $E\cup P'$.

(c) Let $A_k$ and $B_l$ be the sums of $\alpha$ over $P_k$ and of $\beta$ over $P'_l$.  Adding the two
congruences~\eqref{eq:partition} gives $A_k\equiv B_l$, so $A_1\equiv A_2\equiv A_3$ on $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$, and also
on its limits, since $\ket{\Psi_{\delta_n}(\alpha_n,\beta_n)}\to\ket{\Psi_1(\alpha)}$ forces $\alpha_n\to\alpha$.
Conversely, let $A_1\equiv A_2\equiv A_3$ and $P'_l=\{w,x,y,z\}$.  Each of its six pairs lies in one mixed block, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed} prescribes the six pair sums $q_{wx}\equiv\beta_w+\beta_x$, and so on.  By
Eq.~\eqref{eq:partition}, the sums of complementary pairs, $q_{wx}+q_{yz}$, $q_{wy}+q_{xz}$ and $q_{wz}+q_{xy}$, are
$A_1$, $A_2$, $A_3$ in some order, hence equal.  Therefore the solutions on $P'_l$ are exactly
$2\beta_w\equiv q_{wx}+q_{wy}-q_{xy}$ and $\beta_x\equiv q_{wx}-\beta_w$, $\beta_y\equiv q_{wy}-\beta_w$,
$\beta_z\equiv q_{wz}-\beta_w$: two solutions, differing by $\pi$ on all of $P'_l$, and eight in total.  Adding $\pi$ on
one quartet changes $\omega$ by $3\pi$, so four of the eight lie in each component, and for each of them
$\ket{\Psi_\delta(\alpha,\beta)}\to\ket{\Psi_1(\alpha)}$ as $\delta\to1$.  At $\delta=-1$ the same holds with $\alpha$
and $\beta$ exchanged.

(d) If $D\ket\Phi$ were real for some $\ket\Phi$ in $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$ and a diagonal local unitary $D$, it would
have $\omega\in\{0,\pi\}$; but it is AME with support $E\cup P\cup P'$, so $\omega\equiv\pm\pi/2$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Branches through the real state.}  At $\alpha=0$ all pair sums in the proof of (c) are $q\equiv\pi$, so $\beta$
is constant on each quartet $P'_l$ with $2\beta\equiv\pi$, i.e.\ $\beta=\eta_l\pi/2$ with $\eta_l=\pm1$.  The eight
straight lines $\delta\mapsto\ket{\Psi_\delta(0,\beta)}$ are the \emph{branches} through $\ket\psi=\ket{\Psi_1(0)}$;
since $\omega=3(\eta_1+\eta_2+\eta_3)\pi/2\equiv\eta_1\eta_2\eta_3\pi/2$, four lie in $\mathcal F_{72}^+$ and four in
$\mathcal F_{72}^-$.  Along a branch only the moduli change: as $\delta$ decreases from $1$ to $-1$, the coefficients on
$P$ shrink from $1/6$ to $0$ and those on $P'$ grow from $0$ to $1/6$.  At $\delta=-1$ a branch ends at
$\ket{\Psi_{-1}(\beta)}$ on the opposite face.  A common phase on $P'$, which a diagonal local unitary applies without
changing the coefficients on $E$, makes this state real; for constant $\beta$ it becomes $\ket{\psi_-}$.  Up to diagonal
local unitaries, the eight branches thus end at four real states, each reached by one branch from each component:
$\ket{\psi_-}$ and the three states obtained from it by negating the coefficients on $P'_1$, $P'_2$ or $P'_3$ (classes
$9$, $9$, $10$, $9$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:classes}).  No permutation of the parties and of the basis states of each party
maps one face onto the other.

\emph{Uniform states.}  Halfway along a branch, at $\delta=0$, all $72$ coefficients have modulus $1/\sqrt{72}$; for
constant $\beta=\pm\pi/2$ they are $\varepsilon_x$, $1$ and $\pm i$ times $1/\sqrt{72}$ on $E$, $P$ and $P'$.  In every
reshaping $W=\sqrt2\,U$ then has two entries from $\{\pm1,\pm i\}$ in each row and column and $WW^\dagger=2\,\mathbb I$:
a 2-unitary complex weighing matrix (Fig.~\ref{fig:amplitudes72}).  These uniform states are new, since the AME$(4,6)$
states reported before have at least $112$ non-zero coefficients (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).  Other uniform states of
$\mathcal F_{72}^+$ are almost real: up to a diagonal local unitary, $\mathcal F_{72}^+$ contains a uniform state with
coefficients $\pm1/\sqrt{72}$ except for $\pm i/\sqrt{72}$ on the four states of $P'_1$, and a path of AME states in
$\mathcal F_{72}^+$ joins it to $\ket\psi$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:families})~\cite{repo}.  Four imaginary coefficients are the
minimum.  If all coefficients of an AME state with support $E\cup P\cup P'$ lie in $\{\pm1,\pm i\}/\sqrt{72}$, then by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:congruence} every block contains an even number of imaginary coefficients, while $\omega\equiv\pm\pi/2$
requires an odd number among the $18$ states of $P\cup P'$ with $x_1\neq0$.  The smallest sets with both properties are
the six quartets~\cite{repo}.

The first-order deformations of an AME state, modulo the tangent space of its local-unitary orbit, form a
local-unitary invariant.  Their dimension is $10$ for a generic member of $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$, $11$ for a generic face
state, and $14$ at $\ket\psi$: $11$ directions along the face and $3$ spanned by the eight branches (numerical ranks,
with gaps of $10^{-15}$ against $10^{-2}$).  The real state is thus a singular point of the AME conditions, where the
$11$-dimensional face and both $10$-dimensional components meet (Fig.~\ref{fig:families}).

\section{Classification of the real states}\label{sec:real}

\emph{Where the real states lie.}  None of them lies in $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:families}(d)); they lie on the two faces.  Up to diagonal local unitaries, $\mathcal F_{60}$ is
the $11$-torus of the phases $\alpha$ modulo a common phase and the sign change of $P_3$, which a diagonal local
unitary with entries $\pm1$ performs without changing the coefficients on $E$; its origin is $\ket\psi$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:blocks}, a face state is real up to diagonal local unitaries if and only if its phases are $0$ or $\pi$
modulo local phases, i.e.\ if and only if it is a point of order at most two of this torus.  The real states of
$\mathcal F_{60}$ are therefore exactly its $2^{11}=2048$ points of order at most two: $1024$ with all
$\alpha_x\in\{0,\pi\}$, and $1024$ with $\alpha_x=\pm\pi/2$ on $P_3$ and $\alpha_x\in\{0,\pi\}$ on $P_1\cup P_2$.  On
$\mathcal F_{60}'$, with origin $\ket{\psi_-}$, the phases $\beta$ are determined modulo a common phase alone, and its
$2048$ real states are those with all $\beta_x\in\{0,\pi\}$.  The closures of $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$ meet
$\mathcal F_{60}$ only in the $9$-torus $\mathcal C$ on which the quartet sums of $\alpha$ agree
(Proposition~\ref{prop:families}(c)), and $\mathcal F_{60}'$ only in the corresponding $9$-torus $\mathcal C'$.  The $2^9=512$ points of order two of each are the real states that are
limits of $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$; the other $3072$ real states lie only on the faces.  The eight straight branches through a
real state of $\mathcal C$ end at real states of $\mathcal C'$ only for the $128$ states of class~$1$ below, the class
of $\ket\psi$ (at states of classes $9$ and $10$); for the other $384$ they end at non-real states~\cite{repo}.

Equivalently, a real state of $\mathcal F_{60}$ is given by signs that solve the $36$ congruences of the $E$-blocks over
$\mathbb F_2$, and likewise for $\mathcal F_{60}'$: on each face there are $2^{32}$ such sign patterns, and modulo
diagonal local unitaries they give the $2048$ real states.  Negating a coefficient $1/6$ keeps a state AME, since these are $1\times1$ blocks.  On each
face, the permutations of the parties and of the basis states $\ket0,\dots,\ket5$ of each party that preserve the support and its officers form a group of order
$192$, with $16$ orbits on the real states of $\mathcal F_{60}$ (eight of size $64$, eight of size $192$) and $24$ on
those of $\mathcal F_{60}'$ (four of size $32$, $20$ of size $96$).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:classes}
The $4096$ real AME$(4,6)$ states with support $E\cup P$ or $E\cup P'$ fall into exactly $20$ classes under local
unitaries and permutations of the parties: eight on $\mathcal F_{60}$ and $12$ on $\mathcal F_{60}'$.  Their
representatives are listed in Table~\ref{tab:classes}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{At most $20$.}  States in one orbit are related by monomial local unitaries and party permutations.  On each
face, orbits are further joined by the real local orthogonal map $H_{12}^{\otimes4}$, a $2\times2$ Hadamard rotation of
the basis states $\ket1,\ket2$ of every party, followed by a monomial map.  This leaves eight classes on $\mathcal F_{60}$ and
$12$ on $\mathcal F_{60}'$~\cite{repo}.\\
\emph{At least $20$.}  Let $J$ be the real Jacobian of the map $\psi\mapsto(\rho_A)_{|A|=2}$ to all six two-party
marginals, and $n(t)$ the number of eigenvalues of $72J^TJ$ below $t$.  A local unitary acts orthogonally on the state
and on each marginal, and a party permutation permutes the marginals, so the spectrum of $J^TJ$, and hence $n(t)$, is
invariant under both.  The pairs $\bigl(n(149/8),n(3/2)\bigr)$ of the $20$ representatives are pairwise different
(Table~\ref{tab:classes}).  To certify them, note that $72J^TJ$ has entries in $\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]$ and splits into
blocks of size at most $160$.  For each block $G$ we form $X=V^T(G-t\mathbb I)V$ in ball arithmetic~\cite{Arb2017},
with $V$ the numerical eigenvectors of $G$, and check that every Gershgorin interval of $X$ excludes $0$.  By
Sylvester's law of inertia, the number of negative intervals is then the number of eigenvalues of $G$ below $t$.
\end{proof}

Simpler invariants do not separate the classes: the power traces $\mathrm{Tr}(J^TJ)^k$, $k\le8$, are equal on
classes $3$--$8$ (numerical).
The real states are isolated: modulo local orthogonal maps, no continuous family of real AME states passes through
any of them.  Most orbits have no real first-order deformation; for the remaining six (two on $\mathcal F_{60}$, four
on $\mathcal F_{60}'$) the two real first-order deformations are obstructed at second order (numerical)~\cite{repo}.
Within the families of Sec.~\ref{sec:families} the real states are therefore isolated points of complex continua.

The deformation dimensions also separate our states from the known solutions: $14$ and $11$ for the real classes and
$10$ for $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$, against $4$ for the golden state of Ref.~\cite{RBB22} and $3$ for the 2-unitary Hadamard
matrix $H(0)$ of Ref.~\cite{BZ24}.  None of our states is locally equivalent to those (numerical ranks, with gaps of
$10^{-15}$ against $10^{-2}$).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The real states have $60$ non-zero coefficients, against $112$ for the golden state~\cite{RBB22,RRKL23}, the sparsest
previously reported, whose representation of minimal R\'enyi-2 decomposition entropy has $144$~\cite{Ram25}.  The
argument of Sec.~\ref{sec:state} also bounds the support of any AME$(4,6)$ state from below.  A coefficient of modulus
$1/6$ exhausts the norm of its row and column in every reshaping, so the classical officers form a code of length $4$
and minimum distance $3$ over six symbols.  Such a code has at most $34$ codewords: $36$ would be a pair of orthogonal
Latin squares of order six, and $35$ complete uniquely to $36$.  Every other row needs at least two entries, so the
support is at least $72-t\ge38$, where $t$ is the number of classical officers.  Whether the gap between $38$ and $60$
can be closed is open.

Further questions remain.  Are there real AME$(4,6)$ states outside these families, and do the real states admit a
form with all amplitudes of modulus $1/36$, i.e.\ a real-equivalent 2-unitary Hadamard matrix?  Such a form would have
to keep the $12$ product states of maximal overlap $1/6$.  The eigenspace of $u^{\otimes4}$ also contains AME states
outside the families: about one in eight search hits lies on a $3$-parameter family of complex AME states with $348$
of the $360$ coefficients non-zero and deformation dimension $3$, which we could not bring to a sparse or real form
(numerical); whether it is related to the Hadamard family of Ref.~\cite{BZ24} is open.  Finally, a real 2-unitary of
order $36$ is a real perfect tensor: a real dual-unitary gate, and a building block of holographic codes with real
amplitudes~\cite{PYHP2015,BKP2019}.
