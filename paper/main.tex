\begin{abstract}
Absolutely maximally entangled states of four six-level systems, AME$(4,6)$, are the quantum solutions of Euler's
problem of the thirty-six officers.  All solutions reported so far are complex, and it has been conjectured that no
real orthogonal 2-unitary matrix of order $36$ exists.  We give real AME$(4,6)$ states with $60$ non-zero
coefficients in $\{\pm1,\sqrt2\}/\sqrt{72}$.  They lie in the $-1$
eigenspace of an order-$4$ local symmetry, and on its support the AME conditions become linear.  We derive two closed-form families of AME$(4,6)$ states: a $10$-parameter family that
%contains 2-unitary complex weighing matrices and
is supported on $72$ basis states, and an $11$-parameter family on $60$ basis states, that meet at the real state.  At each of its two ends the first family loses twelve coefficients, and the AME states on the remaining $60$ basis states
form an $11$-parameter family: the second family at one end, and an analogous family at the other.  Together these two contain $4096$ real AME states up to local phases, $1024$ of which are limits of the first family.  They fall into exactly $20$ classes under local unitaries and permutations of the parties, and we certify their separation with interval arithmetic.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A pure state of $N$ parties with $d$ levels each is absolutely maximally entangled, AME$(N,d)$, if every reduction
to $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ parties is maximally mixed~\cite{Helwig2012,GALRZ15}.  Such states are perfect tensors; they
underlie quantum secret sharing~\cite{Helwig2012} and holographic codes~\cite{PYHP2015}, and 2-unitary matrices
are dual-unitary gates of maximal entangling power~\cite{RAL20,BKP2019}.  For four parties, writing $U_{(ij),(kl)}=d\,\psi_{ijkl}$, the state is AME if and only if
$U$ is \emph{2-unitary}: $U$, its realignment $U^R$ and its partial transpose $U^\Gamma$ are all
unitary~\cite{GALRZ15}.  Up to permutations of rows and columns, these three matrices are the reshapings of
$d\,\psi$ along the three bipartitions $12|34$, $13|24$ and $14|23$.

A pair of orthogonal Latin squares of order $d$ gives a 2-unitary permutation matrix.  Such pairs exist for every
$d\ge3$ except $d=6$~\cite{BSP1960}: Euler's problem of the $36$ officers has no
solution~\cite{Euler1782,Tarry1900}.  AME$(4,2)$ states do not exist at all~\cite{HS2000}.  An AME$(4,6)$ state, a
quantum solution of Euler's problem, was found by Rather \emph{et al.}~\cite{RBB22}.  Further solutions include
continuous families obtained from it~\cite{RRKL23}, 2-unitary complex Hadamard matrices~\cite{BZ24}, constructions
from biunimodular vectors~\cite{Rat24} and hand-made solutions~\cite{GG25}.  No pure stabilizer AME$(4,6)$ state
exists~\cite{WMBA26,Cha26}, and every quantum solution requires entanglement: mutually orthogonal quantum Latin
squares of order six without entanglement do not exist~\cite{BS26}.

All of these solutions are complex.  In the Supplemental Material of Ref.~\cite{RBB22} the authors write:
``Further numerical results suggest that any 2-unitary matrix $U_{36}$ has complex entries.  Therefore, it is
tempting to conjecture that there exists no solution in the set $O(36)$ of orthogonal matrices of this size.''  A
recent review lists the question as open problem (T2)~\cite{RBRLZ26}: ``Is there an AME(4,6) state with real
coefficients? Equivalently, is there an orthogonal 2-unitary matrix of order 36?''

We answer this question in the affirmative.  With the permutation solutions for $d\neq2,6$, it follows that a real
AME$(4,d)$ state exists if and only if $d\ge3$, exactly when a complex one does.  Our results are the following.
(i)~An explicit real AME$(4,6)$ state with $60$ non-zero coefficients, the sparsest AME$(4,6)$ state we know of,
whose 2-unitaries are direct sums of $1\times1$ and $2\times2$ blocks (Sec.~\ref{sec:state}).
(ii)~Its origin: it lies in the $-1$ eigenspace of the local symmetry
$u^{\otimes4}$, $u=\mathrm{diag}(i,-1,-1,-i,-i,-i)$, and on its support the AME conditions are linear
(Sec.~\ref{sec:symmetry}).
(iii)~Two continuous families of AME$(4,6)$ states in closed form that meet at the real state; one contains
2-unitary complex weighing matrices (Sec.~\ref{sec:families}).
(iv)~A complete classification of the real AME states with the two sixty-term supports at the ends of the family: $4096$ states
in exactly twenty classes under local unitaries and party permutations, all of them isolated (numerically;
Sec.~\ref{sec:real}).
All statements are verified by self-contained scripts~\cite{repo}.

\section{A real AME(4,6) state}\label{sec:state}

If we index the basis states of each party as $\ket{i}$, where $i = 0,\dots,5$, then we can write the state as array of Euler's officers where the first two
parties carry rank $a$ and regiment $b$, and the last two parties carry the state $\ket{\phi_{ab}}$ attached to them:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state}
\sqrt{72}\,\ket\psi=\sum_{a,b=0}^{5}\ket{ab}\otimes\ket{\phi_{ab}}\,.
\end{equation}

The $36$ states $\ket{\phi_{ab}}$, for one officer each, are listed in Table~\ref{tab:state}.
The $12$ shaded cells are \emph{classical} officers: a single
basis state with coefficient $\sqrt2/\sqrt{72}=1/6$.  The other $24$ are \emph{entangled} officers, each a
superposition of two basis states with coefficients $\pm1/\sqrt{72}$.  The support of $\ket\psi$ thus has
$12+2\cdot24=60$ basis states $\ket x=\ket{x_1x_2x_3x_4}$, where $x_j\in\{0,\dots,5\}$ is the index of party $j$.
With $\psi_x=\langle x|\psi\rangle$ we write
\[
P=\{x:\psi_x=1/6\},\qquad E=\{x:\psi_x=\pm1/\sqrt{72}\}
\]
for the $12$ basis states of the classical officers ($P$) and the $48$ of the entangled ones ($E$), and $\psi_x=\varepsilon_x/\sqrt{72}$ with $\varepsilon_x=\pm1$ for $x\in E$ and $\varepsilon_x=\sqrt{2}$ for $x\in P$.  For $x\in P$ all four indices $x_j$ lie in $\{0,3,4,5\}$ and exactly one of them is $0$, e.g.\ $\ket{0344}$;
for $x\in E$ exactly two of them lie in $\{1,2\}$, e.g.\ $\ket{0012}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:state}
$\ket\psi$ is an AME$(4,6)$ state: six times each of its three reshapings is a real orthogonal 2-unitary matrix of
order $36$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a bipartition and let $U$ be the corresponding reshaping of $6\psi$.  Each row and each column of $U$ contains
either one entry $1$ or two entries $\pm1/\sqrt2$, and rows with two entries come in pairs that use the same two
columns.  After permuting rows and columns, $U$ is therefore a direct sum of $12$ blocks $(1)$ and $12$ blocks
$\frac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}s_1&s_2\\ s_3&s_4\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ with $s_i=\pm1$ and
$s_1s_3+s_2s_4=0$, all orthogonal.  For $12|34$ the rows of $U$ are the states $\ket{\phi_{ab}}/\sqrt2$, and two cells
of Table~\ref{tab:state} with the same two kets form a $2\times2$ block; e.g.\ $(a,b)=(0,0)$ and $(5,5)$ give
$\frac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&1\\ 1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.  The rows of the other two reshapings
collect the basis states $\ket x$ with given indices $(x_1,x_3)$ or $(x_1,x_4)$; their block lists are part of the exact check in
Ref.~\cite{repo}.  Orthogonality of the three reshapings is 2-unitarity, which is equivalent to
$\rho_{AB}=\mathbb I/36$ for all pairs of parties.
\end{proof}

All one- and two-party marginals have also been verified in exact arithmetic over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$~\cite{repo};
Fig.~\ref{fig:amplitudes} shows the three 2-unitaries.  In each of the three reshapings the rows, the officers of
Ref.~\cite{RBB22}, are again the $12$ classical officers, the basis states of $P$, and $24$ entangled officers, Bell-type
states $(\ket{kl}\pm\ket{k'l'})/\sqrt2$ of rank and regiment with $k\neq k'$ and $l\neq l'$; in the golden solution all
$36$ officers are entangled~\cite{RBB22}.  The classical officers also make $\ket\psi$ as close to a product
state as an AME$(4,6)$ state can be: for every product state $\ket v=\ket{v_1v_2v_3v_4}$,
$|\langle v|\psi\rangle|^2\le\langle v_1v_2|\rho_{12}|v_1v_2\rangle=1/36$, with equality at the $12$ states of $P$.

\section{Symmetry and linearization}\label{sec:symmetry}

The state was found by a search that takes only $N=4$ and $d=6$ as input.  We looked for AME states in eigenspaces
of local symmetries $U^{\otimes4}$, $U=VDV^\dagger$, with $V$ Haar random and $D$ diagonal of finite order.  Two
necessary conditions restrict the spectrum of $D$: a matching condition on the spectra of $U_A$ and $U_{A^c}$ for every
cut, and the feasibility of a linear program for the populations $|\psi_x|^2$ in the eigenbasis.  Up to order six,
$121$ of the $204$ symmetry classes that pass the first condition also pass the second~\cite{repo}.  Let
$u=\mathrm{diag}(i^{k_0},\dots,i^{k_5})$ with $k=(1,2,2,3,3,3)$.  The $-1$ eigenspace of $u^{\otimes4}$ is spanned by
the $360$ basis states $\ket{x_1x_2x_3x_4}$ with $k_{x_1}+\dots+k_{x_4}\equiv2\pmod4$, and a random-start optimization
in it finds AME states within a few restarts.  The commutant of $u$, $U(1)\times U(2)\times U(3)$ on the basis states
$\ket0$; $\ket1,\ket2$; and $\ket3,\ket4,\ket5$ of each party, preserves the eigenspace.  Minimizing a smoothed $\ell_1$ norm over local frames in
the commutant brings a typical solution to exactly $72$ non-zero coefficients, and a Gauss--Newton polish on that
support makes it exact~\cite{repo}.  These $72$ basis states form an orthogonal array of strength $2$ and index
$2$~\cite{HSS1999}: for any two parties $j\neq k$ and any $m,m'\in\{0,\dots,5\}$, exactly two of the $72$ basis states
have $x_j=m$ and $x_k=m'$.

On such a support the AME conditions linearize.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:blocks}
Let $S\subset\{0,\dots,5\}^4$ be such that, in each bipartition $\{a,b\}|\{c,d\}$, the bipartite graph with an edge from row
$(x_a,x_b)$ to column $(x_c,x_d)$ for every $x\in S$ has only components of size $1\times1$ and $2\times2$.  A state with support exactly $S$ and
coefficients $\sqrt{s_x/72}\,e^{i\varphi_x}$ is AME if and only if $s_x=2$ on every $1\times1$ block and, on every
$2\times2$ block $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}w&x\\ y&z\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:congruence}
\begin{gathered}
s_w=s_z,\quad s_x=s_y,\quad s_w+s_x=2,\\
\varphi_w-\varphi_x-\varphi_y+\varphi_z\equiv\pi\pmod{2\pi}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In particular, if the moduli equations have a positive solution, there is a real AME state with support $S$ if and
only if the congruences are solvable over $\mathbb F_2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each reshaping of $6\psi$ is the direct sum of its blocks and is unitary if and only if every block is.  A $1\times1$
block is unitary if and only if its entry has modulus $1$.  A $2\times2$ block is unitary if and only if its rows and
columns have unit norm, which gives the three linear equations, and its columns are orthogonal, which, with all four
entries non-zero, is the phase condition.  For phases in $\{0,\pi\}$ the congruences are linear equations over
$\mathbb F_2$.
\end{proof}

All $72$ points of the sparse support satisfy $\sum_jk_{x_j}\equiv2\pmod4$, so every state supported there, including
$\ket\psi$ and both families below, satisfies $u^{\otimes4}\ket\psi=-\ket\psi$.  Modulo local phases, the diagonal local
unitaries that act on the support as a scalar form the group $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_4$, in which
$u^{\otimes4}$ has order $4$.

\section{Two families through the real state}\label{sec:families}

Let $P'$ be the $12$ further basis states listed in Appendix~\ref{app:K}; as for $P$, all four indices of each $x\in P'$
lie in $\{0,3,4,5\}$ and exactly one of them is $0$.  The $72$ states $E\cup P\cup P'$ form the orthogonal array above.  In each
bipartition they split into $18$ blocks of size $2\times2$: the $12$ blocks of $E$, and six blocks with two states of
$P$ on one diagonal and two of $P'$ on the other.  Let $K\in\{0,\pm1\}^{72\times9}$ be the matrix of
Appendix~\ref{app:K} and $(K\theta)_x=\sum_jK_{xj}\theta_j$.  For $\delta\in[-1,1]$, $\theta\in\mathbb R^9$ and
$\sigma=\pm1$ let
\begin{multline}\label{eq:F72}
\ket{\Psi_\sigma(\delta,\theta)}=\frac1{\sqrt{72}}\Big[\sum_{x\in E}\varepsilon_xe^{i(K\theta)_x}\ket x\\
+\sqrt{1+\delta}\sum_{x\in P}e^{i(K\theta)_x}\ket x\\
+i\sigma\sqrt{1-\delta}\sum_{x\in P'}e^{i(K\theta)_x}\ket x\Big],
\end{multline}
and let
\begin{multline}\label{eq:F60}
\ket{\Phi(\theta,\theta_{10},\theta_{11})}=\frac1{\sqrt{72}}\sum_{x\in E}\varepsilon_xe^{i(K\theta)_x}\ket x\\
+\frac16\sum_{x\in P}e^{i(K\theta)_x+i\theta_{10}[x=3505]+i\theta_{11}[x=5045]}\ket x ,
\end{multline}
where $[\cdot]$ is the indicator.  Then $\ket\psi=\ket{\Phi(0)}=\ket{\Psi_\pm(1,0)}$.  At $\delta=\pm1$ twelve
coefficients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:F72} vanish.  We call the AME states with support exactly $E\cup P$ or $E\cup P'$ the two
\emph{faces}: $\mathcal F_{60}$, described by Eq.~\eqref{eq:F60}, and $\mathcal F_{60}'$, reached at $\delta=-1$.  We
write $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$ for the states~\eqref{eq:F72} with $|\delta|<1$ and $\sigma=\pm1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:families}
(a) Every $\ket{\Psi_\sigma(\delta,\theta)}$ is AME.  Up to diagonal local unitaries, every AME state with support
exactly $E\cup P\cup P'$ lies in $\mathcal F_{72}^+$ or $\mathcal F_{72}^-$.  These are the two connected components of
that set; complex conjugation exchanges them, and they have $10$ parameters modulo local unitaries.\\
(b) Every $\ket{\Phi(\theta,\theta_{10},\theta_{11})}$ is AME.  Up to diagonal local unitaries, every AME state with
support exactly $E\cup P$ is of this form: the moduli are rigid and the phases form one $11$-dimensional torus.\\
(c) $\ket{\Psi_\sigma(1,\theta)}=\ket{\Phi(\theta,0,0)}$, and a state of $\mathcal F_{60}$ is a limit of
$\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$ if and only if $\theta_{10},\theta_{11}\in2\pi\mathbb Z$.  The states of $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$
that converge to $\ket\psi$ have one of exactly eight limiting phase patterns on $P'$, realized by the eight straight
branches $\delta\mapsto\ket{\Psi_\sigma(\delta,\theta)}$ of Table~\ref{tab:branches}, four in each component.\\
(d) No AME state with support exactly $E\cup P\cup P'$ is real, even up to diagonal local unitaries.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Everything follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:blocks} by integer linear algebra.  On $E\cup P\cup P'$ the moduli equations
have the one-parameter solution $s=1$, $1+\delta$, $1-\delta$ on $E$, $P$, $P'$.  The $54$ congruences have rank
$42$; their real kernel, of dimension $30$, is spanned by the columns of $K$ and $21$ independent diagonal local
phases.  The Smith normal form has a single invariant factor $2$, so the solution set has two components, containing
$\sigma=\pm1$.  On $E\cup P$ the states of $P$ are $1\times1$ blocks; the $36$ remaining congruences have a
$32$-dimensional kernel and all invariant factors $1$.  For~(c), the $P'$-phases of a limit must solve the $54$
congruences with the face phases fixed, which constrains only $\theta_{10}$ and $\theta_{11}$; with the phases of
$\ket\psi$ fixed, the $54\times12$ system has invariant factors $1^92^3$, hence exactly eight solutions, all with
phases $\pm\pi/2$.  For~(d), the congruences on $E\cup P\cup P'$ have no solution over $\mathbb F_2$.  The AME property
of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:F72} and~\eqref{eq:F60} for all parameters has also been checked symbolically~\cite{repo}.
\end{proof}

At $\delta=0$ each branch passes through a state with uniform amplitudes $1/\sqrt{72}$.  For $\theta=0$ its
coefficients are $\varepsilon_x$, $1$ and $i\sigma$ on $E$, $P$ and $P'$, so in every reshaping $W=\sqrt2\,U$ has
entries in $\{0,\pm1,\pm i\}$, two per row and column, and $WW^\dagger=2\,\mathbb I$: these are 2-unitary complex
weighing matrices.  As $\delta$ grows to $1$, the weight of each entry of $P'$ moves to its partner in $P$ until
the state becomes real at $\ket\psi$.  At $\delta=-1$ the roles of $P$ and $P'$ are exchanged, and each of the eight branches
through $\ket\psi$ ends, up to diagonal local unitaries, at a real state of $\mathcal F_{60}'$; the simplest ends at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psiminus}
\ket{\psi_-}=\frac1{\sqrt{72}}\sum_{x\in E}\varepsilon_x\ket x+\frac16\sum_{x\in P'}\ket x .
\end{equation}
No permutation of the parties and of the basis states of each party maps one face onto the other.

The first-order deformations of an AME state, modulo the tangent space of its local-unitary orbit, form a
local-unitary invariant.  Their dimension is $10$ for a generic member of $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$, $11$ for a generic face
state, and $14$ at $\ket\psi$: $11$ directions along the face and $3$ spanned by the eight branches (numerical ranks,
with gaps of $10^{-15}$ against $10^{-2}$).  The real state is thus a singular point of the AME conditions, where the
$11$-dimensional face and both $10$-dimensional components meet (Fig.~\ref{fig:families}).

\section{Classification of the real states}\label{sec:real}

\emph{Where the real states lie.}  None of them lies in $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:families}(d)); they lie on the two faces.  Up to diagonal local unitaries, $\mathcal F_{60}$ is
the torus $\mathbb R^{11}/\Lambda$ of the parameters $(\theta,\theta_{10},\theta_{11})$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:F60}, with
origin $\ket\psi$, where $\Lambda$ is the lattice of parameters that change the state only by diagonal local
unitaries.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:blocks}, a face state is real up to diagonal local unitaries if and only if its phases
are $0$ or $\pi$ modulo local phases, i.e.\ if and only if twice its parameter lies in $\Lambda$.  The real states of
$\mathcal F_{60}$ are therefore exactly its $2^{11}=2048$ points of order at most two.  Since $\Lambda$ strictly
contains $2\pi\mathbb Z^{11}$, with $\Lambda/2\pi\mathbb Z^{11}\cong\mathbb Z_2^3$, only $256$ of them have all
$\theta_j\in\{0,\pi\}$.  The same holds for $\mathcal F_{60}'$, with origin $\ket{\psi_-}$ and
$\Lambda/2\pi\mathbb Z^{11}\cong\mathbb Z_2^2$.  The closures of $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$ meet $\mathcal F_{60}$ only in the
$9$-torus $\mathcal C=\{\theta_{10}=\theta_{11}=0\}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:families}(c)) and $\mathcal F_{60}'$ only in
the corresponding $9$-torus $\mathcal C'$.  The $2^9=512$ points of order two of each are the real states that are
limits of $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$; the other $3072$ real states lie only on the faces.  The eight straight branches through a
real state of $\mathcal C$ end at real states of $\mathcal C'$ only for the $128$ states of class~$1$ below, the class
of $\ket\psi$ (at states of classes $9$ and $10$); for the other $384$ they end at non-real states~\cite{repo}.

Equivalently, a real state of $\mathcal F_{60}$ is given by signs that solve the $36$ congruences of the $E$-blocks over
$\mathbb F_2$, and likewise for $\mathcal F_{60}'$: on each face there are $2^{32}$ such sign patterns, and modulo
diagonal local unitaries they give the $2048$ real states.  Negating a coefficient $1/6$ keeps a state AME, since these are $1\times1$ blocks.  On each
face, the permutations of the parties and of the basis states $\ket0,\dots,\ket5$ of each party that preserve the support and its officers form a group of order
$192$, with $16$ orbits on the real states of $\mathcal F_{60}$ (eight of size $64$, eight of size $192$) and $24$ on
those of $\mathcal F_{60}'$ (four of size $32$, $20$ of size $96$).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:classes}
The $4096$ real AME$(4,6)$ states with support $E\cup P$ or $E\cup P'$ fall into exactly $20$ classes under local
unitaries and permutations of the parties: eight on $\mathcal F_{60}$ and $12$ on $\mathcal F_{60}'$.  Their
representatives are listed in Table~\ref{tab:classes}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{At most $20$.}  States in one orbit are related by monomial local unitaries and party permutations.  On each
face, orbits are further joined by the real local orthogonal map $H_{12}^{\otimes4}$, a $2\times2$ Hadamard rotation of
the basis states $\ket1,\ket2$ of every party, followed by a monomial map.  This leaves eight classes on $\mathcal F_{60}$ and
$12$ on $\mathcal F_{60}'$~\cite{repo}.\\
\emph{At least $20$.}  Let $J$ be the real Jacobian of the map $\psi\mapsto(\rho_A)_{|A|=2}$ to all six two-party
marginals, and $n(t)$ the number of eigenvalues of $72J^TJ$ below $t$.  A local unitary acts orthogonally on the state
and on each marginal, and a party permutation permutes the marginals, so the spectrum of $J^TJ$, and hence $n(t)$, is
invariant under both.  The pairs $\bigl(n(149/8),n(3/2)\bigr)$ of the $20$ representatives are pairwise different
(Table~\ref{tab:classes}).  To certify them, note that $72J^TJ$ has entries in $\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]$ and splits into
blocks of size at most $160$.  For each block $G$ we form $X=V^T(G-t\mathbb I)V$ in ball arithmetic~\cite{Arb2017},
with $V$ the numerical eigenvectors of $G$, and check that every Gershgorin interval of $X$ excludes $0$.  By
Sylvester's law of inertia, the number of negative intervals is then the number of eigenvalues of $G$ below $t$.
\end{proof}

Simpler invariants do not separate the classes: the power traces $\mathrm{Tr}(J^TJ)^k$, $k\le8$, are equal on

The real states are isolated: modulo local orthogonal maps, no continuous family of real AME states passes through
any of them.  Most orbits have no real first-order deformation; for the remaining six (two on $\mathcal F_{60}$, four
on $\mathcal F_{60}'$) the two real first-order deformations are obstructed at second order (numerical)~\cite{repo}.
Within the families of Sec.~\ref{sec:families} the real states are therefore isolated points of complex continua.

The deformation dimensions also separate our states from the known solutions: $14$ and $11$ for the real classes and
$10$ for $\mathcal F_{72}^\pm$, against $4$ for the golden state of Ref.~\cite{RBB22} and $3$ for the 2-unitary Hadamard
matrix $H(0)$ of Ref.~\cite{BZ24}.  None of our states is locally equivalent to those (numerical ranks, with gaps of
$10^{-15}$ against $10^{-2}$).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The real states have $60$ non-zero coefficients, against $112$ for the golden state~\cite{RBB22,RRKL23}, the sparsest
previously reported, whose representation of minimal R\'enyi-2 decomposition entropy has $144$~\cite{Ram25}.  The
argument of Sec.~\ref{sec:state} also bounds the support of any AME$(4,6)$ state from below.  A coefficient of modulus
$1/6$ exhausts the norm of its row and column in every reshaping, so the classical officers form a code of length $4$
and minimum distance $3$ over six symbols.  Such a code has at most $34$ codewords: $36$ would be a pair of orthogonal
Latin squares of order six, and $35$ complete uniquely to $36$.  Every other row needs at least two entries, so the
support is at least $72-t\ge38$, where $t$ is the number of classical officers.  Whether the gap between $38$ and $60$
can be closed is open.

Further questions remain.  Are there real AME$(4,6)$ states outside these families, and do the real states admit a
form with all amplitudes of modulus $1/36$, i.e.\ a real-equivalent 2-unitary Hadamard matrix?  Such a form would have
to keep the $12$ product states of maximal overlap $1/6$.  The eigenspace of $u^{\otimes4}$ also contains AME states
outside the families: about one in eight search hits lies on a $3$-parameter family of complex AME states with $348$
of the $360$ coefficients non-zero and deformation dimension $3$, which we could not bring to a sparse or real form
(numerical); whether it is related to the Hadamard family of Ref.~\cite{BZ24} is open.  Finally, a real 2-unitary of
order $36$ is a real perfect tensor: a real dual-unitary gate, and a building block of holographic codes with real
amplitudes~\cite{PYHP2015,BKP2019}.
